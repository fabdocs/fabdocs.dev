Fabric activity doesn't live in one place. User and admin actions — viewing
a report, running a notebook, changing a permission, creating an item — land
in the **Microsoft Purview / M365 unified audit log**. Job runs and refreshes
show up separately in the Monitoring hub. CU consumption has its own home in
the Capacity Metrics app. Full source breakdown and retention by source is in
[Audit logs](/docs/governance/audit-logs). The one fact worth isolating from
that table: the unified audit log — the record anyone doing a security
review or incident investigation actually wants — defaults to **180 days**,
longer only with an add-on or E5 license.

## Why 180 days is easy to not notice

Nothing about the portal tells you retention is finite until you go looking
for something older than it. A permission change from eight months ago, a
question about who accessed a regulated workspace last quarter, an
auditor asking for twelve months of history for a certification — any of
these can land on a security team's desk with zero warning that the record
might already be gone. The gap isn't a bug or an outage; it's the default
working exactly as designed, discovered at the worst possible moment because
nothing prompts you to check before you need it.

## The adjacent record that doesn't cover for it

The Capacity Metrics app tracks CU consumption by item and operation, and
it's tempting to treat its activity history as a backup audit trail once
you've noticed the 180-day ceiling elsewhere. It isn't one — it exists for
cost and performance, keeps roughly 14–28 days of detail, and its grain is
coarse (consumption by operation, not "who did what to which object"). If
your actual need is "prove who touched this workspace and when," the
Capacity Metrics app answers a different question entirely, at a shorter
window than the thing you're trying to work around.

## The fix is continuous export, not a longer default

<Callout type="warn">
Don't wait for a retention add-on or an E5 upgrade to solve this. Continuous
export to somewhere you control is the only version of this that doesn't
depend on a license tier staying the same.
</Callout>

Subscribe the Office 365 Management API's `Audit.General` content type and
poll the feed into a SIEM — Sentinel, Splunk, whatever your team already
runs. This is the supported path for continuous ingestion, as opposed to the
Purview portal's manual search, which is fine for a one-off investigation
but isn't something you can schedule or alert on.

For regulated workspaces specifically, pair this with **workspace
monitoring**: enabling it provisions an Eventhouse (a KQL database) that
Fabric streams engine-level diagnostic logs into — Spark, semantic model,
Eventhouse activity — with a retention policy you set yourself, not one
Microsoft sets for you. That's the piece that actually lets you promise "we
keep 400 days of this" instead of "we keep whatever the current default
happens to be."

## What a compliance-ready setup actually requires

1. **Continuous export** of the unified audit log to a SIEM — not reliance
   on 180-day portal retention, ever, regardless of license tier.
2. **Workspace monitoring enabled on every regulated workspace**, with an
   explicit Eventhouse retention policy matching your actual requirement.
3. **Alerting in the SIEM**, not the portal, on permission changes, workspace
   identity changes, and OneLake security role edits — the portal doesn't
   alert, it only lets you search after the fact.
4. **A quarterly access review** that joins "who actually read what" against
   current role membership, with the evidence exported somewhere that
   outlives the audit log's own retention window.

The through-line: anything that only lives in the portal is on a clock you
don't control. Export is the only way to make the clock yours.
